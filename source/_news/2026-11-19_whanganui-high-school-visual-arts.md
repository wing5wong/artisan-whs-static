**Multimedia Journalist·NZ Herald·Rachel Maher**

**Retiring teacher Graham Hall thanks Waiuku College mentors who inspired 50-year career**

**Fifty years after stepping into the classroom as a teacher, Graham Hall found himself thinking about the people who once stood at the front of his class.**

**As his career draws to a close, the Whanganui High School art teacher wanted his old school to know how much those lessons had mattered.**

**Hall penned a letter that moved his former school community, with the college’s principal calling it a powerful reminder of the difference teachers can make.**

**“I feel it is important to acknowledge where that journey began and to recognise those who helped set me on my path,” Hall wrote.**

**Hall, a visual arts teacher, attended Waiuku College between 1969 and 1972.**

**In a letter to Waiuku College’s principal Stuart Kelly, he reflected on how encouragement he received as a teenager carried through a teaching career that began in 1976.**

**“My journey from student to teacher, and ultimately to a career spanning five decades, would not have been possible without the influence, encouragement and inspiration of the teachers I had the privilege of learning from during my time at the college.**

**“Each, in their own way, contributed to shaping my interests, my outlook on life, and ultimately my decision to pursue a career in teaching in the visual arts.”**

**The enthusiasm and commitment he saw in his teachers gave him the drive to keep learning, and an example to carry into classrooms of his own.**

**Frank Bryce, in English, gave him a love of reading. Jerry Kuggeleijn inspired a lifelong interest in physical education, while Jock Whitley nurtured his love of the outdoors.**

**His art teachers, Ted Kindleysides and Alistair Nesbit-Smith, helped shape the interests that would eventually become his profession.**

**Both men were practising artists, and their commitment to making their own work alongside teaching stayed with Hall.**

**“So I not only teach art but make art,” he told the Herald.**

**Hall had always wanted to teach. He went straight from school to training college, then into primary teaching before moving to secondary schools.**

**“I guess I have always been on either side of the \[school] desk.”**

**He wanted to give students more than subject knowledge, to encourage the curiosity his own teachers had nurtured.**

**“Good teachers do far more than deliver a curriculum,” he wrote.**

**“They inspire curiosity, encourage students to recognise their potential, and sometimes plant the seeds of a future that a young person may not yet be able to imagine.”**

**He described a career spent working with students, sharing knowledge and encouraging creativity, hoping to make a difference in their lives, just as his teachers had made a difference in his.**

**“For me, Waiuku College was more than simply the place where I completed my secondary education. It was where some of the foundations of my future were established.”**

**His letter also reminded teachers, who might never hear what became of children they once taught, of the influence they hold.**

**“We may never fully appreciate the difference we make at the time,” Hall wrote, “but the impact can remain with a student for a lifetime”.**

**Hall himself had a glimpse of how his influence had carried into another generation.**

**As he walked up to receive a life membership from an art education association, a voice called out from the audience: “Oh, that’s my teacher!”.**

**It belonged to a former student who had become an art teacher herself.**

**It was a full-circle moment, he said.**

**Kelly said Hall’s letter had touched many in the school community, reflecting the enduring impact schools could have on young people.**

**“His words are a powerful reminder that education is about far more than academic achievement. It is about relationships, encouragement, belonging, and helping students discover their potential.**

**“We are incredibly proud that Graham’s experience at Waiuku College has remained so meaningful to him, and we are honoured that he chose to share his story.”**
